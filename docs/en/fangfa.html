Which steps use zhiqi, which are checked by people, and what happens when we get it wrongHow Yikonglu works: verifying zhiqi-assisted readings of the classics

Yikonglu uses zhiqi (智器, our word for AI) to make videos that read the classics. Zhiqi can invent commentary, invent sources and mispronounce characters, so we set out here which steps use zhiqi, which must be checked by a person, and what we do about mistakes — for readers to check us against, and for others doing similar work.

1. Base editions and the commentators

  • Read everything first: before a book begins, we read through the commentaries, excavated manuscripts and textual variants, build a chapter-by-chapter workbook, and only then fix the format and write. We never start from a single base text, and never use web texts of unknown origin as a base.
  • Analects: the text and Zhu Xi's commentary follow the Sibu Beiyao edition of the Lunyu Jizhu. Older commentaries use public-domain woodblock editions, e.g. Huang Kan's Lunyu Yishu in the Zhibuzu Zhai edition and a 1750 Japanese edition. Modern commentaries still in copyright (Yang Bojun, Qian Mu) are quoted only briefly, with page numbers.
  • Daodejing: the base is Wang Bi's commentary in the Siku Quanshu edition, read chapter by chapter against Heshang Gong, the Mawangdui silk manuscripts A and B, the Guodian bamboo slips, the Peking University Han slips, and Yan Zun, Emperor Xuanzong, Su Zhe and the commentators collected in Jiao Hong's Laozi Yi.
  • Three Character Classic: the text follows Yikonglu's Father's Lecture Notes, checked group by group against Wang Xiang's Sanzijing xungu (1831 Japanese reprint). Groups missing from Wang Xiang's text, and groups worded differently, are marked on the page. Other editions, such as He Xingsi's Sanzijing zhujie beiyao and Zhang Taiyan's revised Sanzijing, are being read in turn.
  • Where each edition comes from: Editions & licenses.

2. How each chapter is made

  1. Text: entered character by character from the base edition; variants go in a variants table; nothing is silently changed.
  2. Pronunciation: each episode has a reading table; polyphonic and rare characters are fixed from the commentaries and standard readings, checked before narration, and compared line by line with speech recognition afterwards; mismatches are re-recorded.
  3. Quotations: every quotation in a video is logged with source and page and checked word for word against the original page before it goes on screen; anything we cannot find on the page is only paraphrased, never shown as a quote.
  4. Original pages: when we cite a commentator, the scanned page goes on screen with the quotation boxed in red; the chapter page on this site shows the same page.
  5. Page images online: every chapter comes with the base edition's page. Tap any sentence of the text and a red box marks it on the page. Each page image carries “Yikonglu yikonglu.com” with the book, volume and page in the corner; please keep it when you reuse the image.
  6. Chapter numbers and sources: all three books number chapters the same way, “Analects 2.1”, “Daodejing 1”, “Three Character Classic 1”, matching the address, e.g. yikonglu.com/lunyu/2.1. Copying a line of the text adds the chapter number and address automatically.
  7. Cross-references to the Analects: the Analects is our main classic. Every chapter of the other books is read against it in three kinds: lines the Analects states directly (source), commentators who cite the Analects to explain (cited by commentators), and lines that share its sense (related). “Related” is our own reading, labelled “Yikonglu's reading”, never passed off as a commentator's.
  8. Renderings and readings: we write our own plain renderings and check them line by line against standard translations, rewriting any match of six characters or more; “How Yikonglu reads it” is our own reading, labelled as ours, never passed off as a commentator's.
  9. Visuals: illustrations and characters are our own; type is cut from the base edition's glyphs and machine-scanned for damage; fictional characters in the videos do not assert “history” beyond the text.

4. When we get it wrong

  • Errors in content (text, quotations, sources, pronunciation, readings), whether reported by readers or found by us, all go on the public corrections page, stating what we said, what it now says and why, and crediting the reader by their chosen name.
  • Published videos are not deleted; where we can, we correct the description, and a remade version is published separately and noted on the corrections page.
  • Our contact address is on the about page.

5. Numbers we publish

Numbers are kept per book, and units differ: the Analects and the Daodejing count chapters, the Three Character Classic counts lectures. “Draft-to-final changes” compares each episode's earliest narration script with its final one segment by segment; any difference in wording counts as a change.

Recently added

  1. 6.4Can someone of bad background be employed?A calf of a brindled plough-ox, pure red with well-formed horns: even if people will not sacrifice it because of its origin, the spirits of hills and rivers will not reject it. A father's badness cannot cancel a son's goodness; Zhu Xi notes specifically that this speaks about Zhonggong, not to his face.
  2. 6.3Why did Confucius give one man little grain and another a lot?How much to give depends not on favour but on what is right. Zihua's family rode fat horses and wore light furs, not in need, so he gave only a little, refusing to add to the rich; Yuan Si's salary was owed by his post, and refusing it would be wrong, so any excess should go to his neighbours. One case is “help the needy, not the rich”; the other is “a regular salary should not be declined”.
  3. 6.2Why is Confucius' “love of learning” not about reading a lot?Duke Ai asks about learning; Confucius answers with two matters of self-cultivation. Anger arises with the matter and stops when it is over, not moved on to others; a fault made once is known and not made again. Saying that since Yan Hui's death he has heard of no one who loves learning shows that love of learning lies in governing one's feelings, not in reading much
  4. 6.1Why can one's affairs be simple but not one's heart?Two kinds of “simple”: one with a master within, reverent everywhere, so affairs are naturally uncluttered; the other first intends to save effort and lets even oneself off, which becomes too simple. Confucius grants Zhonggong the south-facing seat; Zhonggong uses the question about Bozi to draw the distinction, and Confucius says “Yong's words are right.” Today: the team with the fewest rules is the one whose leader is the most punctual and orderly.
  5. 5.27The one thing Confucius claimed to have more ofLoyalty and trust are the base, found everywhere; love of learning is effort, and rare. Confucius does not boast of innate knowledge, only of loving to learn, to encourage others. The Gongye Chang book ends here
  6. 5.26Why is it so hard to see one's own faults?Two levels: knowing one's fault is already rare; knowing it and blaming oneself inwardly is rarer still; only self-arraignment leads to thorough change. Confucius' sigh of “never seen” is a warning to learners

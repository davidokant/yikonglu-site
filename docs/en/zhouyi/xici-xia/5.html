Great Commentary II, chapter 5 · Great Commentary IIThe Changes says

English translation

The Changes says: "Restless, coming and going; only your friends follow your thoughts." The Master said: "What is there under heaven to think about, to deliberate over? All under heaven comes to the same end by different roads, to one goal through a hundred deliberations. What is there under heaven to think about, to deliberate over?"

When the sun goes, the moon comes; when the moon goes, the sun comes; sun and moon displace each other, and light is produced. When cold goes, heat comes; when heat goes, cold comes; cold and heat displace each other, and the year is completed. What goes contracts; what comes expands; contraction and expansion act on each other, and benefit is produced.

The measuring worm contracts in order to stretch out; dragons and snakes hibernate in order to preserve themselves. Refining meaning until one enters the spirit-like is for the sake of application; benefiting application and keeping oneself at ease is for the sake of exalting virtue.

Beyond this, no one yet knows. To fathom the spirit-like and understand transformation is the fullness of virtue.

The Changes says: "Hemmed in by rock, leaning on thorns; he enters his house and does not see his wife: misfortune." The Master said: "To be hemmed in by what should not hem one in means one’s name will surely be disgraced; to lean on what should not be leaned on means one’s person will surely be endangered. Disgraced and endangered, the time of death is at hand; how could he get to see his wife?"

The Changes says: "The duke shoots a hawk on top of a high wall; he gets it: nothing that is not favorable." The Master said: "The hawk is the bird; bow and arrow are the implements; the one who shoots is the man. The noble person keeps his implements on his person and waits for the right time to act; how could it not be favorable? He acts without hindrance, and so he goes out and makes his catch. This speaks of acting with implements ready."

The Master said: "The petty person is not ashamed of being inhumane and does not fear being unrighteous; without seeing gain he is not stirred, without being threatened he is not corrected. A small correction that brings a great warning is the petty person’s good fortune. The Changes says, ‘His feet in the stocks, his toes covered: no blame.’ This is what it means."

If good is not accumulated, it is not enough to make a name; if evil is not accumulated, it is not enough to destroy oneself. The petty person thinks a small good is of no benefit and does not do it, and thinks a small evil does no harm and does not give it up; so his evil accumulates until it cannot be hidden, and his crime grows until it cannot be pardoned. The Changes says: "Carrying the cangue, his ears covered: misfortune."

The Master said: "Danger comes to one who felt secure in his position; ruin comes to one who felt sure of his survival; disorder comes to one who felt he had order. Therefore the noble person, when secure, does not forget danger; when surviving, does not forget ruin; when in order, does not forget disorder. Thus his person is secure and his state can be preserved. The Changes says: ‘It may perish, it may perish! Tie it to a clump of mulberry.’"

The Master said: "Virtue slight but position honored, wisdom small but plans great, strength little but burden heavy: few escape disaster. The Changes says: ‘The cauldron’s leg breaks, the duke’s stew is overturned, heavy punishment: misfortune.’ This speaks of not being equal to the task."

The Master said: "To know the incipient: that is spirit-like! The noble person does not flatter those above him and is not overfamiliar with those below him: is this not knowing the incipient? The incipient is the slightest beginning of movement, the first appearance of good fortune. The noble person sees the incipient and acts, without waiting for the day to end. The Changes says: ‘Firm as a rock; not waiting for the day to end; constancy brings good fortune.’ Firm as a rock, why wait for the day to end? It can be decided and known. The noble person knows the subtle and the manifest, knows the yielding and the firm: he is the hope of ten thousand men."

The Master said: "The son of the Yan family is nearly there! When he had a fault, he never failed to recognize it; having recognized it, he never repeated it. The Changes says: ‘Returning from not far away; not coming to regret: supreme good fortune.’"

Heaven and earth intermingle closely, and the myriad things are transformed and made rich; male and female join their essences, and the myriad things are transformed and born. The Changes says: "When three people travel together, they lose one; when one person travels alone, he finds his companion." This speaks of attaining oneness.

The Master said: "The noble person makes his person secure before he acts, calms his heart before he speaks, settles his relationships before he asks. The noble person cultivates these three, and so he is whole. If he acts from danger, the people will not side with him; if he speaks from fear, the people will not respond; if he asks without a relationship, the people will not give. When no one sides with him, those who would harm him arrive. The Changes says: ‘No one increases him; someone strikes him; he sets his heart without constancy: misfortune.’"

Zhu Xi's chapter note

The above is the fifth section.

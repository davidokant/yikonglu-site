Great Commentary I, chapter 1 · Great Commentary IHeaven is lofty and honored, earth is low

English translation

Heaven is lofty and honored, earth is low; thus Qian and Kun are fixed. Low and high being set out, the noble and the mean have their places. Movement and rest have their constancy; thus the firm and the yielding are distinguished. Affairs gather by kind and things divide by groups; thus good fortune and misfortune arise. In heaven images are formed, on earth shapes are formed; thus change and transformation appear.

Therefore the firm and the yielding rub against each other, and the eight trigrams set one another in motion.

Things are roused by thunder and lightning and moistened by wind and rain; sun and moon revolve, and cold and heat alternate.

The way of Qian forms the male; the way of Kun forms the female.

Qian presides over the great beginning; Kun brings things to completion.

Qian presides by ease; Kun accomplishes by simplicity.

What is easy is easily understood; what is simple is easily followed. Being easily understood, it wins affection; being easily followed, it achieves results. With affection it can endure; with results it can grow great. To endure is the virtue of the worthy; to grow great is the achievement of the worthy.

With ease and simplicity the principles of all under heaven are grasped; with the principles of all under heaven grasped, one takes one's place in their midst.

Zhu Xi's chapter note

The above is the first section.

Translated by Yikonglu, following Zhu Xi's reading.

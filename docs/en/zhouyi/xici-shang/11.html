Great Commentary I, chapter 11 · Great Commentary IThe Master said

English translation

The Master said: "What is the Changes for? The Changes opens up things and completes affairs; it covers the way of all under heaven, and that is all. Therefore the sages use it to penetrate the aims of all under heaven, to settle the undertakings of all under heaven, and to resolve the doubts of all under heaven.

Therefore the virtue of the yarrow stalks is round and spirit-like; the virtue of the hexagrams is square and wise; the meaning of the six lines is to change so as to inform. With these the sages cleansed their hearts, withdrew and hid in secrecy, and shared the people's concern over good fortune and misfortune. By spirit they knew what was to come, by wisdom they stored what had passed. Who could achieve this? Only the ancients, keen of hearing and sight, wise and knowing, divinely martial yet not killing!

Thus they understood the way of heaven and examined the circumstances of the people, and raised up the spirit-like things to go before the people's use. With these the sages fasted and took precautions, so as to make their virtue spirit-like and bright!

Therefore closing the door is called Kun; opening the door is called Qian. One closing and one opening is called change; going and coming without end is called penetration. What appears is called image; what takes shape is called implement. Making and using it is called method; what benefits going out and coming in, and what all the people use, is called spirit.

Therefore in the Changes there is the Supreme Ultimate, which produces the two modes; the two modes produce the four images; the four images produce the eight trigrams.

The eight trigrams determine good fortune and misfortune; good fortune and misfortune give rise to the great undertaking.

Therefore of models and images none is greater than heaven and earth; of change and penetration none is greater than the four seasons; of images hung on high and shining bright none is greater than sun and moon; of the exalted none is greater than wealth and rank. In preparing things for use and establishing implements for the benefit of the world, none is greater than the sages. In probing the hidden and seeking out the concealed, hooking up the deep and reaching the distant, so as to fix good fortune and misfortune for the world and spur the world to diligent effort, none is greater than yarrow and tortoise.

Therefore heaven produced spirit-like things and the sages took them as models; heaven and earth change and transform and the sages imitated them; heaven hangs out images that show good fortune and misfortune and the sages imaged them; the River brought forth the Chart and the Luo brought forth the Writing, and the sages took them as models.

The Changes has the four images to show; it attaches texts to inform; it fixes them with good fortune and misfortune to decide."

Zhu Xi's chapter note

The above is the eleventh section.

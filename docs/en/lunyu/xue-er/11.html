Xue er (Book 1) · chapter 11While the father lives, observe the son's intent

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: Change what is wrong at once; let what can wait, wait. That is time given to what one cannot yet bear to let go. Today: a son inherits his father's small factory. The old rule that cheated customers he scraps the next day; another he never liked he leaves for three years, not because it is right, but because he still can't let it go. The common view is “Even if the father was wrong, change nothing for three years”; Yikonglu's reading puts the weight elsewhere.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：父亲在世时，看儿子的志向；父亲去世后，看儿子的行为；三年里不急着改父亲的做法，可以算孝了。

常The common reading

Even if the father was wrong, change nothing for three years.

注Key words in the commentary

“父在，子不得自专，而志则可知；父没，然后其行可见” — while the father lives the son cannot act on his own, but his intent can be known; after the father's death his conduct can be seen.

说What other commentators say

Yin Tun: “如其道，虽终身无改可也；如其非道，何待三年” — if the way was right, never change it; if it was wrong, why wait three years? You Zuo: it refers to “在所当改而可以未改者耳” — things that ought to be changed but can wait.

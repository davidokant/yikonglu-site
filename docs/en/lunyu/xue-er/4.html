Xue er (Book 1) · chapter 4Three things I examine myself on daily

What this chapter says

When you act for someone, did you hold anything back? Today: a colleague asks you to look over a plan; you can't be bothered to point out the flaw and say “looks good”.

白Plain Chinese

曾子说：我每天从三件事上反省自己：替人办事，有没有尽心尽力？和朋友交往，有没有不实在？老师传授的，有没有熟习于自己？

常The common reading

Reflect on yourself many times a day; zhong means loyalty to a person.

注Key words in the commentary

“尽己之谓忠，以实之谓信” — giving one's all is zhong, being true to the facts is xin; “传谓受之于师，习谓熟之于己” — chuan is what one receives from a teacher, xi is making it one's own; “三者之序，以忠信为传习之本” — in the order of the three, zhong and xin are the root of what is passed on and practised.

说What other commentators say

One reading in He Yan's Jijie takes chuan as what one passes on to others (teaching before one has mastered it); Yang Bojun reads san as “many times” rather than “three”.

Xue er (Book 1)

  1. 1Learning and practising it in season
  2. 2Filial and fraternal duty as the root of ren
  3. 3Clever words and a pleasing face
  4. 4Three things I examine myself on daily
  5. 5Governing a state of a thousand chariots
  6. 6A young man at home is filial
  7. 7Honouring the worthy over good looks
  8. 8A gentleman without weight has no dignity
  9. 9Care at the end, remembrance of the distant
  10. 10Whenever the Master arrives in a state
  11. 11While the father lives, observe the son's intent
  12. 12In the use of ritual, harmony is prized
  13. 13Trust that comes close to what is right
  14. 14A gentleman does not seek to eat his fill
  15. 15Poor without flattery
  16. 16Not troubled that others do not know me

Li ren (Book 4) · chapter 26Importune a ruler and you meet disgrace; importune a friend and you grow distant

白Plain Chinese

子游说：侍奉君主过于烦琐，就会招致羞辱；对待朋友过于烦琐，就会被疏远。

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

常The common reading

Read as “don't keep bothering your boss or friends”, or reading shu (数) as “to scold”.

注Key words in the commentary

Shuo (数) read shuò, frequent. Zhu Xi cites the Cheng brothers: “数，烦数也” — shuo means tiresome frequency; and Hu Yin: “事君谏不行，则当去；导友善不纳，则当止” — when remonstrance with a ruler is not acted on, one should leave; when guiding a friend to good is not accepted, one should stop.

家What other commentators say

Fan Zuyu: “君臣朋友，皆以义合，故其事同也” — ruler and minister, friend and friend, are all joined by rightness, so the matter is the same. These are Ziyou's words.

读How Yikonglu reads it

Persuasion has limits. When it does not get through, stop (with a ruler, leave); urging too often only pushes people away.

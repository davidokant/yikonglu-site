Li ren (Book 4) · chapter 2The inhumane cannot long endure hardship

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: Long hardship makes people overstep, long pleasure makes them indulge; whether outside things can take a person away depends on whether the original heart is there. Today: someone out of work for six months still gets up at seven, shaves, and goes out for a run. The common view is “Poverty can corrupt people and so can wealth; judge people over the long run”; Yikonglu's reading puts the weight elsewhere.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：没有仁德的人，不能长久地处在穷困中，也不能长久地处在安乐中。有仁德的人安于仁，聪明的人深知仁的好，一心要得到它。

常The common reading

Poverty can corrupt people and so can wealth; judge people over the long run.

注Key words in the commentary

“乐”音洛 — le read luo; “约，穷困也” — yue means poverty and hardship; “利，犹贪也，盖深知笃好而必欲得之也” — li means craving: knowing it deeply, loving it earnestly, and determined to have it; “不仁之人，失其本心，久约必滥，久乐必淫” — the inhumane have lost their original heart: long in hardship they run wild, long in pleasure they overindulge; “惟仁者则安其仁而无适不然，知者则利于仁而不易所守……皆非外物所能夺矣” — the humane rest in humaneness wherever they are, and the wise benefit from it and do not change what they hold to; nothing outside can take it from them.

说What other commentators say

Xie Liangzuo: “仁者心无内外远近精粗之间，非有所存而自不亡，非有所理而自不乱，如目视而耳听，手持而足行也” — for the humane there is no gap of inner and outer, near and far, coarse and fine; nothing is held yet nothing is lost, nothing is arranged yet nothing is disordered, like eyes seeing, ears hearing, hands holding and feet walking; “安仁则一，利仁则二” — resting in humaneness is one, profiting from it is two (the passage “知者谓之有所见则可……未能无意也” in between is left out of the video).

Compared with Zhu Xi

CommentaryPointRelationConfidenceNote
杨伯峻《论语译注》samefairly sureThe inhumane cannot long endure poverty or ease; the humane rest in ren, the wise profit by ren. Zhu explains by the original heart and firm love; Yang's translation explains 利 by ren's effects.
钱穆《论语新解》samefairly sureBoth gloss 约 as hardship; resting in ren is natural, profiting by ren is knowing ren is good and wanting it. Neither reads it as using ren for outside gain; Qian further speaks of a settled state of mind.

Li ren (Book 4)

  1. 1Living among the humane is beautiful
  2. 2The inhumane cannot long endure hardship
  3. 3Only the humane can love and hate others
  4. 4Set your heart on humaneness and there is no evil
  5. 5Wealth and rank are what people want
  6. 6I have not seen one who loves humaneness
  7. 7People's faults each follow their kind
  8. 8Hear the Way in the morning, die content at dusk
  9. 9A scholar set on the Way who is ashamed of poor clothes
  10. 10No fixed yes, no fixed no; side with what is right
  11. 11The gentleman cherishes virtue, the petty man his comfort
  12. 12Act by what profits you, and resentment multiplies
  13. 13Can one govern a state with ritual and yielding?
  14. 14Worry not about having no post, but about what you stand on
  15. 15My Way is threaded through by one
  16. 16The gentleman understands rightness; the petty man understands profit
  17. 17Seeing a worthy, think of equalling him; seeing an unworthy, look inward
  18. 18In serving parents, remonstrate gently
  19. 19While parents live, do not travel far; if you travel, have a fixed direction
  20. 20For three years not altering his father's way
  21. 21One must know the ages of one's parents
  22. 22The ancients did not let words out, ashamed that their conduct would not keep up
  23. 23Those who err through restraint are few
  24. 24The gentleman wishes to be slow in speech and quick in action
  25. 25Virtue is not solitary; it is sure to have neighbours
  26. 26Importune a ruler and you meet disgrace; importune a friend and you grow distant

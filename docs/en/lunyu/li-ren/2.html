Li ren (Book 4) · chapter 2The inhumane cannot long endure hardship

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：没有仁德的人，不能长久地处在穷困中，也不能长久地处在安乐中。有仁德的人安于仁，聪明的人知道仁对自己有利，便去行仁。

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

常The common reading

Poverty can corrupt people and so can wealth; judge people over the long run.

注Key words in the commentary

“乐”音洛 — le read luo; “约，穷困也” — yue means poverty and hardship; “利，犹贪也，盖深知笃好而必欲得之也” — li means craving: knowing it deeply, loving it earnestly, and determined to have it; “不仁之人，失其本心，久约必滥，久乐必淫” — the inhumane have lost their original heart: long in hardship they run wild, long in pleasure they overindulge; “惟仁者则安其仁而无适不然，知者则利于仁而不易所守……皆非外物所能夺矣” — the humane rest in humaneness wherever they are, and the wise benefit from it and do not change what they hold to; nothing outside can take it from them.

家What other commentators say

Xie Liangzuo: “仁者心无内外远近精粗之间，非有所存而自不亡，非有所理而自不乱，如目视而耳听，手持而足行也” — for the humane there is no gap of inner and outer, near and far, coarse and fine; nothing is held yet nothing is lost, nothing is arranged yet nothing is disordered, like eyes seeing, ears hearing, hands holding and feet walking; “安仁则一，利仁则二” — resting in humaneness is one, profiting from it is two (the passage “知者谓之有所见则可……未能无意也” in between is left out of the video).

读How Yikonglu reads it

Long hardship makes people overstep, long pleasure makes them indulge; whether outside things can take a person away depends on whether the original heart is there. Today: someone out of work for six months still gets up at seven, shaves, and goes out for a run.

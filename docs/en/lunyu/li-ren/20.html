Li ren (Book 4) · chapter 20For three years not altering his father's way

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: The half sentence must be read with the half before it; “do not alter for three years” cannot be taken alone as an iron rule. Today: when young we remember only half of what our parents said, and recall the other half only after running a household ourselves. The common view: Taken alone, read as “after a father dies, nothing may be changed for three years”. Yikonglu's reading puts the weight elsewhere.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：如果三年里不急着改掉父亲的做法，可以算是孝了。

常The common reading

Taken alone, read as “after a father dies, nothing may be changed for three years”.

注Key words in the commentary

Zhu Xi cites Hu Yin: “已见首篇，此盖复出而逸其半也” — this already appears in the first book; here it is repeated and has lost its first half. The chapter repeats Xue Er 1.11 without its opening, “父在，观其志；父没，观其行” (while the father lives, observe his intent; after the father dies, observe his conduct).

说What other commentators say

Under Xue Er 1.11, Zhu Xi cites Yin Tun and You Zuo: what is not in accord with the Way need not wait three years; what can be changed but can wait is left a while, to give the unbearing heart time (this episode revisits that original page).

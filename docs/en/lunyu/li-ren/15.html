Li ren (Book 4) · chapter 15My Way is threaded through by one

What this chapter says

“Loyalty and reciprocity” do not say the Way in full; they hand the learner something to hold. “Threaded through by one” is said only to the one who is ready to understand it. Today: an old craftsman tells the apprentice just one thing: do your own step through to the end, then think of the person who takes over next.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：参啊，我的道是由一个根本贯穿起来的。曾子说：是。孔子出去以后，同学们问：这是什么意思？曾子说：老师的道，就是忠和恕，尽在这里了。

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

常The common reading

Is “loyalty and reciprocity” the whole content of Confucius's Way? And is “loyalty” loyalty to a ruler?

注Key words in the commentary

Shen (参) read shen, wei (唯) read in the rising tone; Zhu Xi: “贯，通也。唯者，应之速而无疑者也” — guan means to run through; wei is a quick answer without doubt; “尽己之谓忠，推己之谓恕” — giving one's whole self is loyalty, extending oneself to others is reciprocity; another reading: “中心为忠，如心为恕” — the heart at the center is zhong, a heart like one's own is shu.

家What other commentators say

Zhu Xi: “圣人之心，浑然一理，而泛应曲当，用各不同。曾子于其用处，盖已随事精察而力行之，但未知其体之一尔” — the sage's mind is one whole principle, responding fitly everywhere with different uses; Zengzi had already examined and practiced the uses, but did not yet know the oneness of the substance; “曾子有见于此而难言之，故借学者尽己、推己之目以著明之” — Zengzi saw this and found it hard to say, so he borrowed the learner's terms “give one's whole self” and “extend oneself” to make it plain. Cheng Yi: “圣人教人各因其才……惟曾子为能达此” — the sage teaches each according to his talent; only Zengzi could reach this.

Li ren (Book 4)

  1. 1Living among the humane is beautiful
  2. 2The inhumane cannot long endure hardship
  3. 3Only the humane can love and hate others
  4. 4Set your heart on humaneness and there is no evil
  5. 5Wealth and rank are what people want
  6. 6I have not seen one who loves humaneness
  7. 7People's faults each follow their kind
  8. 8Hear the Way in the morning, die content at dusk
  9. 9A scholar set on the Way who is ashamed of poor clothes
  10. 10No fixed yes, no fixed no; side with what is right
  11. 11The gentleman cherishes virtue, the petty man his comfort
  12. 12Act by what profits you, and resentment multiplies
  13. 13Can one govern a state with ritual and yielding?
  14. 14Worry not about having no post, but about what you stand on
  15. 15My Way is threaded through by one
  16. 16The gentleman understands rightness; the small man understands profit
  17. 17Seeing a worthy, think of equalling him; seeing an unworthy, look inward
  18. 18In serving parents, remonstrate gently
  19. 19While parents live, do not travel far; if you travel, have a fixed direction
  20. 20For three years not altering his father's way
  21. 21One must know the ages of one's parents
  22. 22The ancients did not let words out, ashamed that their conduct would not keep up
  23. 23Those who err through restraint are few
  24. 24The gentleman wishes to be slow in speech and quick in action
  25. 25Virtue is not solitary; it is sure to have neighbours
  26. 26Importune a ruler and you meet disgrace; importune a friend and you grow distant

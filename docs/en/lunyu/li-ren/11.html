Li ren (Book 4) · chapter 11The gentleman cherishes virtue, the petty man his comfort

What this chapter says

“Cherishing one's ground” is not homesickness; it is a heart that guards only its own little comfort. The whole difference between gentleman and petty man lies between public and private. Today: two people receive the same job offer in another city; one asks first what he can learn and whom he can help, the other only which place is more comfortable to stay in.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：君子怀念的是道德，小人怀念的是乡土；君子关心的是法度，小人关心的是恩惠。

常The common reading

Is “cherishing one's ground” just homesickness, and is the petty man simply a bad person?

注Key words in the commentary

“怀，思念也。怀德，谓存其固有之善。怀土，谓溺其所处之安。怀刑，谓畏法。怀惠，谓贪利” — huai means to keep in mind; cherishing virtue means preserving the good one already has; cherishing one's ground means being sunk in the comfort of where one is; cherishing the law means fearing the law; cherishing favors means craving gain; “君子小人趣向不同，公私之间而已矣” — gentleman and petty man differ in where they tend, and that is only the line between public and private.

说What other commentators say

Yin Tun: “乐善恶不善，所以为君子；苟安务得，所以为小人” — delighting in good and hating what is not good makes the gentleman; settling for ease and striving for gain makes the petty man.

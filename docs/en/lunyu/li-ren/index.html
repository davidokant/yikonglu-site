Analects, book 4 · 26 of 26 chaptersLi ren (Book 4)

Each chapter gives the source text, the key words in the commentary, what other commentators say, and Yikonglu's reading, with the video.

  1. 4.1Living among the humane is beautifulChoose the wrong place to live and what you lose is not the house but the heart that tells right from wrong
  2. 4.2The inhumane cannot long endure hardshipLong hardship makes people overstep, long pleasure makes them indulge; whether outside things can take a person away depends on whether the original heart is there
  3. 4.3Only the humane can love and hate othersEveryone can like and dislike; the “can” lies in doing it rightly, which requires a heart that is not tied to anything
  4. 4.4Set your heart on humaneness and there is no evilResolve does not guarantee no mistakes; it guarantees no intention to do wrong
  5. 4.5Wealth and rank are what people wantDo not take wealth that should not come, and do not dodge an undeserved poverty by crooked means; the big choices and the small daily work make each other
  6. 4.6I have not seen one who loves humanenessWhere the heart goes, the strength goes; the hard part is not strength but willingness
  7. 4.7People's faults each follow their kindWhether a fault leans too generous or too mean shows whether one is humane
  8. 4.8Hear the Way in the morning, die content at duskThe hard part is not hearing it but truly believing it once heard; only one who has really gained something can say “I could die that evening content.” Today: an old man in a hospital bed finally understands what he never could all his life, holds his daughter's hand and says “now I'm at peace.”
  9. 4.9A scholar set on the Way who is ashamed of poor clothesThe mouth says the heart is set on the Way, but the heart is ordered about by things outside. The problem is not dressing poorly; it is treating poor dress as a shame
  10. 4.10No fixed yes, no fixed no; side with what is rightDo not decide in advance and do not refuse in advance; look only at whether it accords with what is right. “Nothing allowed, nothing forbidden” without rightness in charge is mere recklessness
  11. 4.11The gentleman cherishes virtue, the petty man his comfort“Cherishing one's ground” is not homesickness; it is a heart that guards only its own little comfort. The whole difference between gentleman and petty man lies between public and private
  12. 4.12Act by what profits you, and resentment multipliesThe resentment does not come from the word “profit”; it comes from profiting only oneself
  13. 4.13Can one govern a state with ritual and yielding?Yielding is the lining inside ritual. When only the outward form is left, ritual itself no longer works, never mind governing a state
  14. 4.14Worry not about having no post, but about what you stand onIt is not about finding ways to be noticed; it is about first having the substance worth noticing. Whether a post is given, whether others know you, is not in your hands
  15. 4.15My Way is threaded through by one“Loyalty and reciprocity” do not say the Way in full; they hand the learner something to hold. “Threaded through by one” is said only to the one who is ready to understand it
  16. 4.16The gentleman understands rightness; the small man understands profitBoth sides use the same kind of “understanding”; the difference is which thing they have understood through and through
  17. 4.17Seeing a worthy, think of equalling him; seeing an unworthy, look inwardThe people one sees are good and bad; each time, fold it back onto oneself
  18. 4.18In serving parents, remonstrate gentlyNot keeping silent, but speaking gently, and if it doesn't get through, not falling out, just trying again another time
  19. 4.19While parents live, do not travel far; if you travel, have a fixed directionIf you can avoid travelling far, do; if you must go, let your parents know where you are
  20. 4.20For three years not altering his father's wayThe half sentence must be read with the half before it; “do not alter for three years” cannot be taken alone as an iron rule
  21. 4.21One must know the ages of one's parentsJoy and fear are the same fact: one more day lived is one day fewer left
  22. 4.22The ancients did not let words out, ashamed that their conduct would not keep upThe fear is not of saying something wrong but of failing to do it
  23. 4.23Those who err through restraint are fewHolding back words, temper and display are all restraint, not only money
  24. 4.24The gentleman wishes to be slow in speech and quick in actionOf the easy end and the hard end, rein in the easy one (speaking) and spur on the hard one (doing). It is not refusing to speak, but not talking loosely.
  25. 4.25Virtue is not solitary; it is sure to have neighboursThis is not “good deeds are rewarded”; it is like answering like. What you hold to draws people who hold to the same thing.
  26. 4.26Importune a ruler and you meet disgrace; importune a friend and you grow distantPersuasion has limits. When it does not get through, stop (with a ruler, leave); urging too often only pushes people away.

Li ren (Book 4) · chapter 5Wealth and rank are what people want

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: Do not take wealth that should not come, and do not dodge an undeserved poverty by crooked means; the big choices and the small daily work make each other. Today: the bus brakes hard, and the young man's first grab is to steady the old woman beside him. A common question is ““Not by the proper way” read the same both times — then why not escape a poverty one did not deserve” — the line above is Yikonglu's answer.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：富和贵，是人人想要的，不用正当的方法得到，就不接受。贫和贱，是人人厌恶的；即使是不该遭受却落到自己身上的，也不违背道义去摆脱。君子离开了仁，凭什么成就君子的名声？君子连吃一顿饭的工夫也不离开仁，匆忙的时候是这样，颠沛流离的时候也是这样。

常The common reading

“Not by the proper way” read the same both times — then why not escape a poverty one did not deserve?

注Key words in the commentary

wu (hate) in the departing tone; “不以其道得之，谓不当得而得之” — “not by the proper way” means getting what one should not get; “于富贵则不处，于贫贱则不去，君子之审富贵而安贫贱也如此” — he does not keep such wealth and does not dodge such poverty: this is how a gentleman weighs wealth and rests in poverty; wu (how) read wu; “若贪富贵而厌贫贱，则是自离其仁，而无君子之实矣” — to crave wealth and loathe poverty is to leave one's own humaneness, with nothing of the gentleman left; “终食者，一饭之顷。造次，急遽苟且之时。颠沛，倾覆流离之际” — a meal is the time of one meal; zaoci is a moment of haste; dianpei is a time of upheaval and displacement.

说What other commentators say

Zhu Xi at the end of the chapter: “取舍之分明，然后存养之功密；存养之功密，则其取舍之分益明矣” — when the line between taking and refusing is clear, the work of preserving and nurturing becomes close; when that work is close, the line becomes clearer still.

Compared with Zhu Xi

CommentaryPointRelationConfidenceNote
钱穆《论语新解》differsfairly sureQian reads 得之 with what follows, Zhu reads 不以其道得之 together; both explain by getting what one should not, not keeping such wealth, not dodging such poverty: ren does not shift with circumstances.
杨伯峻《论语译注》贫贱得之word sensesettledYang thinks the second 得 should be 去 and translates by the chapter's spirit: not escaping poverty by improper means; Zhu keeps 得, meaning getting what one should not get, never leaving ren in poverty or success.

Li ren (Book 4)

  1. 1Living among the humane is beautiful
  2. 2The inhumane cannot long endure hardship
  3. 3Only the humane can love and hate others
  4. 4Set your heart on humaneness and there is no evil
  5. 5Wealth and rank are what people want
  6. 6I have not seen one who loves humaneness
  7. 7People's faults each follow their kind
  8. 8Hear the Way in the morning, die content at dusk
  9. 9A scholar set on the Way who is ashamed of poor clothes
  10. 10No fixed yes, no fixed no; side with what is right
  11. 11The gentleman cherishes virtue, the petty man his comfort
  12. 12Act by what profits you, and resentment multiplies
  13. 13Can one govern a state with ritual and yielding?
  14. 14Worry not about having no post, but about what you stand on
  15. 15My Way is threaded through by one
  16. 16The gentleman understands rightness; the petty man understands profit
  17. 17Seeing a worthy, think of equalling him; seeing an unworthy, look inward
  18. 18In serving parents, remonstrate gently
  19. 19While parents live, do not travel far; if you travel, have a fixed direction
  20. 20For three years not altering his father's way
  21. 21One must know the ages of one's parents
  22. 22The ancients did not let words out, ashamed that their conduct would not keep up
  23. 23Those who err through restraint are few
  24. 24The gentleman wishes to be slow in speech and quick in action
  25. 25Virtue is not solitary; it is sure to have neighbours
  26. 26Importune a ruler and you meet disgrace; importune a friend and you grow distant

公冶长第五 · chapter 4Yong is humane but not glib

白Plain Chinese

有人说：冉雍有仁德，只是不善言辞。孔子说：要能说会道做什么？凭嘴快去应付人，往往招人厌恶。他算不算仁我不敢说，可要口才做什么呢？

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

常The common reading

Read as “poor speakers lose out, so train your eloquence”.

注Key words in the commentary

“佞，口才也” — ning is a talent for speech; Zhonggong was “重厚简默，而时人以佞为贤” — weighty, simple and quiet, while people of the time thought glibness a virtue; “御，当也，犹应答也。给，辩也。憎，恶也” — yu is to meet, as in replying; ji is ready argument; zeng is to dislike; “佞人所以应答人者，但以口取辩而无情实，徒多为人所憎恶尔” — the glib answer people with argument from the mouth but no real substance, and only win much dislike; “其不佞乃所以为贤，不足以为病也。再言焉用佞，所以深晓之” — his not being glib is exactly his worth, not a flaw; saying “what use is glibness” twice is to make it deeply clear.

家What other commentators say

Zhu Xi on why Confucius would not grant him humaneness: “仁道至大，非全体而不息者，不足以当之。如颜子亚圣，犹不能无违于三月之后” — the way of humaneness is very great; only one whole and unceasing can match it; even Yan Hui, second to the sage, could not avoid lapsing after three months.

读How Yikonglu reads it

The root problem is “no real substance”: a quick, argumentative tongue with nothing true in it wins more dislike the more it talks. Confucius did not grant Zhonggong humaneness because humaneness is too great to grant lightly. Today: the person who takes on everything, agrees to everything and delivers nothing finds colleagues walking around him three months later.

公冶长第五 · chapter 11What I do not wish others to do to me

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: The difference is between “not” and “do not”: having no such wish in the first place is ren; having it and holding yourself back is shu. Today: you hate being woken by messages at midnight, so you schedule anything after 10 pm to send the next morning. The common view: Read as another version of “do not do to others what you would not want done to yourself”. Yikonglu's reading puts the weight elsewhere.

白Plain Chinese

子贡说：我不愿别人加在我身上的事，我也不想加到别人身上。孔子说：赐啊，这还不是你够得着的。

常The common reading

Read as another version of “do not do to others what you would not want done to yourself”, something anyone can do.

注Key words in the commentary

“子贡言我所不欲人加于我之事，我亦不欲以此加之于人。此仁者之事，不待勉强，故夫子以为非子贡所及” — Zigong said that what he did not want others to do to him, he did not want to do to others; this is the work of the humane, needing no effort, so the Master judged it beyond Zigong; “愚谓无者自然而然，勿者禁止之谓，此所以为仁恕之别” — in my view, wu (无, there is none) is what happens of itself, wu (勿, do not) is prohibition; this is the difference between humaneness and reciprocity.

说What other commentators say

The Cheng brothers: “我不欲人之加诸我，吾亦欲无加诸人，仁也；施诸己而不愿，亦勿施于人，恕也。恕则子贡或能勉之，仁则非所及矣” — what I do not want done to me, I also wish there to be none of from me to others: that is humaneness; what one would not want done to oneself, do not do to others: that is reciprocity. Reciprocity Zigong might manage with effort; humaneness is beyond him.

公冶长第五 · chapter 6If the Way does not prevail, I will take a raft out to sea

What this chapter says

Going out to sea is a sigh, a figure of speech; Zilu took it literally, with courage to spare but no sense of measuring what is fitting. Today: someone in the group chat says offhand they'll quit and travel the world, and someone else packs a bag that very night.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：道行不通的话，我就乘个木筏漂到海上去。肯跟我走的，大概是仲由吧？子路听了很高兴。孔子说：仲由好勇胜过我，只是不懂得裁度事理。

常The common reading

Read as Confucius really wanting to retire overseas; or wu suo qu cai (无所取材) as “nowhere to get timber”.

注Key words in the commentary

“桴，筏也” — fu is a raft; hao (好) in the falling tone; “材，与裁同，古字借用” — cai is the same as cai “to measure”, an ancient borrowed character; “子路勇于义，故谓其能从己，皆假设之言耳。子路以为实然……讥其不能裁度事理，以适于义也” — Zilu was brave for rightness, so he was said to be able to follow; these were all hypothetical words. Zilu took them literally, and was chided for being unable to weigh the matter to fit rightness.

说What other commentators say

The Cheng brothers: “浮海之叹，伤天下之无贤君也” — the sigh about floating out to sea is grief that the world has no worthy ruler.

Wei zheng (Book 2) · chapter 15Learning without thinking

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading (一孔按): One is empty, the other hangs in the air. Today: bookmarking online courses without thinking vs. building theories without reading. Zhu Xi's commentary: “不求诸心，故昏而无得” — not seeking it in the heart, one is in the dark and gains nothing (lost); “不习其事，故危而不安” — not practising the matter, one is unsteady and insecure (perilous).

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：只学习不思考，就会迷惘无所得；只思考不学习，就会危殆不安。

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

注Key words in the commentary

“不求诸心，故昏而无得” — not seeking it in the heart, one is in the dark and gains nothing (lost); “不习其事，故危而不安” — not practising the matter, one is unsteady and insecure (perilous).

说What other commentators say

Cheng Yi cites the Doctrine of the Mean — study widely, inquire closely, think carefully, distinguish clearly, practise earnestly: “废其一，非学也” — drop one and it is not learning.

Wei zheng (Book 2) · chapter 24Sacrificing to spirits not one's own

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: Taking gifts to the home of a boss you have nothing to do with; saying nothing in the meeting. Zhu Xi's commentary: “非其所当祭之鬼” — spirits one has no business sacrificing to; “谄，求媚也” — flattery means currying favour; “知而不为，是无勇也” — knowing and not acting is lack of courage.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：不是自己应当祭祀的鬼神，却去祭祀，这是谄媚。看到应该做的事却不去做，这是没有勇气。

说What other commentators say

Zhu Xi says nothing about how the two lines connect; our own reading — “one does what should not be done, the other fails to do what should be done” — is marked as this video's reading, not Zhu Xi's.

Compared with Zhu Xi

CommentaryPointRelationConfidenceNote
杨伯峻《论语译注》samefairly sureBoth warn against sacrificing where one should not, to curry favour, and against seeing right and not doing it. Yang adds that 鬼 usually means ancestors and notes auspicious and mourning rites; this addition is not forced into an opposite reading.
钱穆《论语新解》samefairly sureQian takes it broadly as improper cults, which may include spirits not one's ancestors; Zhu also glosses spirits one should not sacrifice to, so he should not be misread as allowing only “ancestors”.

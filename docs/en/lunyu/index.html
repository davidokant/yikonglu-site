Xue er (Book 1)

  1. 1.1Learning and practising it in seasonWhat you learned, keep taking out to practise and to do
  2. 1.2Filial and fraternal duty as the root of renFilial and fraternal duty is where practising ren begins
  3. 1.3Clever words and a pleasing faceWatch whether he does it to get the thing done, or only to be liked.
  4. 1.4Three things I examine myself on dailyWhen you act for someone, did you hold anything back? Today: a colleague asks you to look over a plan; you can't be bothered to point out the flaw and say “looks good”.
  5. 1.5Governing a state of a thousand chariotsCheng Yi said these five things sound plain, but pushed to the limit, even Yao and Shun governed no differently
  6. 1.6A young man at home is filialFilial and brotherly duty, care and good faith, love for all come first, study of letters after: this is about order, not choosing one over the other
  7. 1.7Honouring the worthy over good looksSomeone who never studied but does all this — Zixia says he has studied
  8. 1.8A gentleman without weight has no dignityZhu Xi takes the words as they stand and does not talk his way around them
  9. 1.9Care at the end, remembrance of the distantPut your heart into what is easy to overlook and easy to forget
  10. 1.10Whenever the Master arrives in a stateComing to ask is not the same as handing you the job.
  11. 1.11While the father lives, observe the son's intentChange what is wrong at once; let what can wait, wait. That is time given to what one cannot yet bear to let go
  12. 1.12In the use of ritual, harmony is prizedHarmony alone, not regulated by ritual, does not work either
  13. 1.13Trust that comes close to what is rightThe work of keeping your word happens before you say yes
  14. 1.14A gentleman does not seek to eat his fillNot going hungry on purpose; simply too occupied to mind.
  15. 1.15Poor without flatteryAcknowledge what has been done, then push one step further
  16. 1.16Not troubled that others do not know meThe book opens with “unrecognised and yet not resentful” and closes here; the two ends meet.

Wei zheng (Book 2)

  1. 2.1Governing by virtueNot that decrees are unneeded, but that the one who governs must first have gained something himself
  2. 2.2Thoughts without crookednessThe “without crookedness” lies in the reader, not wholly in the poems
  3. 2.3Guiding them by decreesPunishments and decrees keep people from crime; virtue and ritual move them toward good
  4. 2.4At fifteen I set my heart on learningNot a certificate issued automatically at a certain age, but a road of learning.
  5. 2.5Meng Yizi asks about filial pietyToday: borrowing money to throw a lavish birthday banquet is “doing what one may not”.
  6. 2.6Meng Wubo asks about filial pietyParents love their children and fear above all that they fall ill, so children should take their parents' heart as their own and look after their health
  7. 2.7Ziyou asks about filial pietyMerely feeding one's parents is no different from keeping dogs and horses; the difference lies in one word, reverence
  8. 2.8Zixia asks about filial piety (the hard part is the face)All the work was done; the face never arrived.
  9. 2.9Hui is not slowWhether someone has learned shows outside the classroom.
  10. 2.10Look at what he doesLook at what he does, examine why he does it, see whether his heart is at ease in it
  11. 2.11Reviewing the old to know the newIf you can only recite it, reheating it a hundred times still gives the same bowl.
  12. 2.12The gentleman is not a vesselVirtue made whole first, then it can be used anywhere, not learning a hundred trades
  13. 2.13Act first, then speakYou still speak — only afterwards.
  14. 2.14Inclusive, not partisanSame dishes on the table, different reasons for the dinner.
  15. 2.15Learning without thinkingLearning without thinking leaves nothing in the heart: empty (wang). Thinking without learning leaves ideas with nowhere to land: hanging (dai)
  16. 2.16Working at strange doctrinesFollowing Zhu Xi: burrowing into it is what does harm.
  17. 2.17To know what you knowAdmitting you do not know is where it starts.
  18. 2.18Zizhang studies to gain officeConfucius promises nothing; he turns seeking office into a lesson in being a person.
  19. 2.19Raise up the straightPeople submit only when the right people are put to use; the hard part is seeing who is straight
  20. 2.20Approach them with dignityJi Kangzi asks for a method; Confucius answers with duty. Treating duty as a method goes wrong again.
  21. 2.21Why do you not take part in government?The words are a pretext, and the point is also true.
  22. 2.22A person without trustworthinessWithout it, the ox is an ox and the cart is a cart; they never connect.
  23. 2.23Can ten generations be known?He watched two handovers and saw what stayed and what changed.
  24. 2.24Sacrificing to spirits not one's ownTaking gifts to the home of a boss you have nothing to do with; saying nothing in the meeting.

Ba yi (Book 3)

  1. 3.1Eight rows of dancers in his courtyardFour extra rows of dancers measure how hard a heart has become
  2. 3.2The three families clear away to the YongThey didn't even understand the song they sang
  3. 3.3A person without humaneness — what of ritual?Fill the eight rows, sing the emperor's ode — if the person is wrong, ritual and music can do nothing with him.
  4. 3.4Lin Fang asks about the root of ritualLavishness and frugality both miss ritual; when both sides are off, lean toward the root
  5. 3.5The Yi and Di have rulersThe rebuke is aimed not at the Yi and Di but at his own people
  6. 3.6The Ji sacrifice to Mount TaiConfucius asks Ran You “can you not save him?”: what needed saving was the Ji clan from the guilt of usurping a rite, not Mount Tai's honour
  7. 3.7The gentleman does not competeThe target does not lie; what is contested is whether your own hand is steady.
  8. 3.8The painting comes after the white groundIf the ground is not smooth, every colour you put on comes out blotchy.
  9. 3.9Not enough recordsConfucius could tell it, but would not rest on his word alone
  10. 3.10The di sacrifice after the libationLooking back, the records and the people are gone; looking around, the rite is there but the heart is not.
  11. 3.11Someone asks about the di sacrificeSaying “I don't know” does not mean he didn't know.
  12. 3.12Sacrifice as if presentThe form of the rite can be laid out by the book; the heart in it has to be brought by the one who sacrifices.
  13. 3.13Better to court the stoveDo the thing right and everyone is easy to deal with; do it wrong and being on good terms with anyone won't help.
  14. 3.14Zhou looked to the two dynastiesFollowing Zhou means following the patient work of watching the forebears improve things bit by bit
  15. 3.15In the Grand Temple he asked about everythingThirty years in, you still double-check.
  16. 3.16In archery, piercing the hide is not the pointThe contest is in what can be trained, not in what you were born with.
  17. 3.17The sheep for announcing the new moonThe sheep marks the rite; keep the marker and the rite can still be recognised and restored
  18. 3.18Serving one's lord with full ritualNot a bit extra — ritual simply requires this; Confucius says only “people take it for flattery”, with no insult back
  19. 3.19The lord employs his ministers with ritualEach side looks only at its own end; Duke Ding was the ruler, and his question was answered with his own half
  20. 3.20The Guanju: joy without excessNot too little feeling, but feeling full without going over
  21. 3.21Duke Ai asks about the altar of the soilThe three “nots” are not “never mind” but a heavy rebuke, to make him careful in future
  22. 3.22Guan Zhong's capacity was smallConfucius does not say outright where the smallness lies; it shows in the luxury and usurpation
  23. 3.23The Master speaks of music to the Grand Music Master of LuFirst together, then harmonious, each part clear within the harmony, linked to the end
  24. 3.24The border warden of Yi asks to see himA minor official saw Confucius's weight at first sight
  25. 3.25The Shao is perfectly beautiful and perfectly goodThe difference is in how it came: one was yielded, one was taken by force
  26. 3.26High position without generosityIt is not that it looks bad; there is nothing left to look at

Li ren (Book 4)

  1. 4.1Living among the humane is beautifulChoose the wrong place to live and what you lose is not the house but the heart that tells right from wrong
  2. 4.2The inhumane cannot long endure hardshipLong hardship makes people overstep, long pleasure makes them indulge; whether outside things can take a person away depends on whether the original heart is there
  3. 4.3Only the humane can love and hate othersEveryone can like and dislike; the “can” lies in doing it rightly, which requires a heart that is not tied to anything
  4. 4.4Set your heart on humaneness and there is no evilResolve does not guarantee no mistakes; it guarantees no intention to do wrong
  5. 4.5Wealth and rank are what people wantDo not take wealth that should not come, and do not dodge an undeserved poverty by crooked means; the big choices and the small daily work make each other
  6. 4.6I have not seen one who loves humanenessWhere the heart goes, the strength goes; the hard part is not strength but willingness
  7. 4.7People's faults each follow their kindWhether a fault leans too generous or too mean shows whether one is humane
  8. 4.8Hear the Way in the morning, die content at duskThe hard part is not hearing it but truly believing it once heard; only one who has really gained something can say “I could die that evening content.” Today: an old man in a hospital bed finally understands what he never could all his life, holds his daughter's hand and says “now I'm at peace.”
  9. 4.9A scholar set on the Way who is ashamed of poor clothesThe mouth says the heart is set on the Way, but the heart is ordered about by things outside. The problem is not dressing poorly; it is treating poor dress as a shame
  10. 4.10No fixed yes, no fixed no; side with what is rightDo not decide in advance and do not refuse in advance; look only at whether it accords with what is right. “Nothing allowed, nothing forbidden” without rightness in charge is mere recklessness
  11. 4.11The gentleman cherishes virtue, the petty man his comfort“Cherishing one's ground” is not homesickness; it is a heart that guards only its own little comfort. The whole difference between gentleman and petty man lies between public and private
  12. 4.12Act by what profits you, and resentment multipliesThe resentment does not come from the word “profit”; it comes from profiting only oneself
  13. 4.13Can one govern a state with ritual and yielding?Yielding is the lining inside ritual. When only the outward form is left, ritual itself no longer works, never mind governing a state
  14. 4.14Worry not about having no post, but about what you stand onIt is not about finding ways to be noticed; it is about first having the substance worth noticing. Whether a post is given, whether others know you, is not in your hands
  15. 4.15My Way is threaded through by one“Loyalty and reciprocity” do not say the Way in full; they hand the learner something to hold. “Threaded through by one” is said only to the one who is ready to understand it
  16. 4.16The gentleman understands rightness; the small man understands profitBoth sides use the same kind of “understanding”; the difference is which thing they have understood through and through
  17. 4.17Seeing a worthy, think of equalling him; seeing an unworthy, look inwardThe people one sees are good and bad; each time, fold it back onto oneself
  18. 4.18In serving parents, remonstrate gentlyNot keeping silent, but speaking gently, and if it doesn't get through, not falling out, just trying again another time
  19. 4.19While parents live, do not travel far; if you travel, have a fixed directionIf you can avoid travelling far, do; if you must go, let your parents know where you are
  20. 4.20For three years not altering his father's wayThe half sentence must be read with the half before it; “do not alter for three years” cannot be taken alone as an iron rule
  21. 4.21One must know the ages of one's parentsJoy and fear are the same fact: one more day lived is one day fewer left
  22. 4.22The ancients did not let words out, ashamed that their conduct would not keep upThe fear is not of saying something wrong but of failing to do it
  23. 4.23Those who err through restraint are fewHolding back words, temper and display are all restraint, not only money
  24. 4.24The gentleman wishes to be slow in speech and quick in actionOf the easy end and the hard end, rein in the easy one (speaking) and spur on the hard one (doing). It is not refusing to speak, but not talking loosely.
  25. 4.25Virtue is not solitary; it is sure to have neighboursThis is not “good deeds are rewarded”; it is like answering like. What you hold to draws people who hold to the same thing.
  26. 4.26Importune a ruler and you meet disgrace; importune a friend and you grow distantPersuasion has limits. When it does not get through, stop (with a ruler, leave); urging too often only pushes people away.

公冶长第五

  1. 5.1Gongye Chang is fit to be given a wifeWhat matters is that the crime was not his; a prison imposed from outside is no measure of his honour
  2. 5.2Of Zijian the Master said: A gentleman indeed is this manA person's virtue is taken from the worthies around him, so praising him means also praising those who made him
  3. 5.3Zigong asked: What about me?Two layers in one line: not yet “not a vessel”, but the most precious kind of vessel; praise with direction in it
  4. 5.4Yong is humane but not glibThe root problem is “no real substance”: a quick, argumentative tongue with nothing true in it wins more dislike the more it talks. Confucius did not grant Zhonggong humaneness because humaneness is too great to grant lightly
  5. 5.5The Master wished Qidiao Kai to take officeWhether one's ability is enough, a teacher can see; the hair-thin part not truly gained inside, only oneself knows. Admitting that hair is refusing to rest in small achievement, so Confucius was glad
  6. 5.6If the Way does not prevail, I will take a raft out to seaGoing out to sea is a sigh, a figure of speech; Zilu took it literally, with courage to spare but no sense of measuring what is fitting
  7. 5.7Meng Wubo asked whether Zilu was humaneSay plainly what visible ability there is; humaneness comes and goes, and one does not vouch for it. Three students, three talents: each line says what they can do, none says they are humane
  8. 5.8Who is better, you or Hui?Confucius's question tests whether Zigong sees himself clearly. He does, and does not find it hard to bow, so Confucius first says “you are not his equal” and then solemnly affirms him
  9. 5.9Zai Yu slept in the daytimeThe point is not the nap but a slackened will and words that deeds do not match. “Not worth rebuking” is the heaviest rebuke. Confucius used the occasion to set a rule: hear the words, and also watch the conduct
  10. 5.10I have never seen anyone who is truly firmFirmness holds things down; desire is being covered by things. Looking hard and loving to win may mean being led by one's own desires
  11. 5.11What I do not wish others to do to meThe difference is between “not” and “do not”: having no such wish in the first place is ren; having it and holding yourself back is shu
  12. 5.12The Master's outward cultivation can be heard“Wenzhang” is virtue showing in words and conduct; Confucius seldom spoke of nature and the Way of Heaven, and Zigong hearing it for the first time is admiration, not complaint
  13. 5.13When Zilu heard somethingWhat he feared was not learning more, but the last thing not yet done. His fellow students recorded it because they felt they fell short
  14. 5.14Why was Kong Wenzi called “wen”?Confucius answers only with the one line of the posthumous-name rules, “quick and fond of learning, not ashamed to ask those below,” without defending Kong Wenzi wholesale
  15. 5.15Zichan had four ways of the gentlemanPraising point by point actually shows there is still somewhere he has not reached. Respectful to himself, reverent to superiors, kind to the people, just in employing them: each of the four has its own object. Judging a person by one phrase or one event is wrong
  16. 5.16Is it more polite not to stand on ceremony with old friends?Respect fading with time is the usual course; keeping it is what “good at associating with others” means
  17. 5.17Was Zang Wenzhong really clever?Not putting his mind to what the people needed, and courting spirits instead, does not count as wisdom
  18. 5.18Not even this counts as ren?Loyalty and purity are single virtues; ren asks whether one fully accords with principle and truly has no selfish mind. Trusting small things to vouch for great ones was Zizhang's fault
  19. 5.19Why is thinking twice enough?By the second time it is already clear; by the third, self-interest creeps in
  20. 5.20Why is this “foolishness” harder to learn than cleverness?The “foolishness” is taking on what every clever person avoids, while still keeping himself safe and saving his lord; Master Cheng's mention of Bi Gan shows it is not simply feigning stupidity
  21. 5.21After so many years of travel, what did Confucius go home to do?He turns from practising the Way to transmitting it; ambitious young men can advance in the Way, but risk overshooting, so he goes home to trim them
  22. 5.22Bo Yi and Shu Qi were so aloof — why did no one resent them?So fastidious they seemed to tolerate no one, yet they stopped once a person changed, so few resented them; the measure of the pure is not keeping old accounts
  23. 5.23Why is borrowing something for a neighbour not upright?Saying “I don't have any” is upright; passing off a borrowed thing as your own is bending to please — a small matter, but Master Cheng says what it harms is uprightness itself
  24. 5.24Why did Confucius find excessive politeness shameful?Both shames are a gap between inside and outside: sweet words, smiles and excessive courtesy to curry favour; befriending someone while nursing a grudge is pretence. Xie says it is worse than theft, because the heart is not upright
  25. 5.25Why was Confucius' wish greater than Zilu's and Yan Yuan's?All three share with others, differing in scale: Zilu shares carriage and horses with friends, Yan Yuan does not boast or show off, Confucius lets each find their place, like heaven and earth nurturing without a trace
  26. 5.26Why is it so hard to see one's own faults?Two levels: knowing one's fault is already rare; knowing it and blaming oneself inwardly is rarer still; only self-arraignment leads to thorough change. Confucius' sigh of “never seen” is a warning to learners
  27. 5.27The one thing Confucius claimed to have more ofLoyalty and trust are the base, found everywhere; love of learning is effort, and rare. Confucius does not boast of innate knowledge, only of loving to learn, to encourage others. The Gongye Chang book ends here

雍也第六

  1. 6.1Why can one's affairs be simple but not one's heart?With a settled heart and respect in everything, affairs become simple of themselves; aiming first at ease and letting yourself off too is too simple
  2. 6.2Why is Confucius' “love of learning” not about reading a lot?Love of learning lies in governing one's feelings: not shifting anger onto others, not making the same mistake twice
  3. 6.3Why did Confucius give one man little grain and another a lot?How much to give depends not on favour but on what is right. Zihua's family rode fat horses and wore light furs, not in need, so he gave only a little, refusing to add to the rich; Yuan Si's salary was owed by his post, and refusing it would be wrong, so any excess should go to his neighbours. One case is “help the needy, not the rich”; the other is “a regular salary should not be declined”.
  4. 6.4Can someone of bad background be employed?A calf of a brindled plough-ox, pure red with well-formed horns: even if people will not sacrifice it because of its origin, the spirits of hills and rivers will not reject it. A father's badness cannot cancel a son's goodness; Zhu Xi notes specifically that this speaks about Zhonggong, not to his face.
  5. 6.5Where did Yan Hui differ from the others?Yan Hui's humaneness is like a host living at home who now and then steps out; the others' is like a guest who drops by now and then. The difference lies in how long it lasts, not in whether it is there.
  6. 6.6Is a single quality enough to take office?Zilu is decisive, Zigong perceptive, Ran Qiu many-skilled: each has one strength and so can be employed. Using people means taking what they are good at.
  7. 6.7Offered an office, why would he flee?He would not become the Ji family's retainer, yet he asked the messenger to decline kindly for him, and if they came again he would cross the river to Qi. Neither defying them head-on nor giving in: too hard invites disaster, too soft invites disgrace.
  8. 6.8Why did Confucius hold his dying student's hand through the window?Boniu's family moved the sickbed under the south window, as for a ruler's visit; Confucius did not dare accept that honour, so he did not enter and held his hand through the window in farewell. “That such a man should have such an illness” is said twice: grief that such a person should suffer such a disease, laid at the door of fate.
  9. 6.9So poor, what was Yan Hui happy about?What he enjoyed was not poverty, but a joy that poverty could not change. Zhu Xi would rather not spell it out, leaving readers to find for themselves what lies in the word “其”.
  10. 6.10He said he lacked the strength: why didn't Confucius believe him?Someone truly lacking strength collapses halfway along the road; Ran Qiu drew a line on the ground before setting out. It is not that he cannot, but that he will not.
  11. 6.11Both are scholars: where is the difference?Studying the same books, one who learns to make something of himself is a gentleman scholar; one who learns to be seen and to gain advantage is a petty scholar. Profit is not only money; reputation counts too.
  12. 6.12Not taking shortcuts: is that talent?He never cut through by-paths and never came to the official's door without public business. Someone who holds firm in small things can be relied on in large ones; Ziyou saw that too when judging people.
  13. 6.13He covered the retreat: why wouldn't he admit it?Holding the rear against the enemy in a rout is great merit, yet as they neared the city gate he whipped his horse and said, “It wasn't that I dared to stay behind; the horse wouldn't go.” Passing the credit to his horse shows a heart with no wish to rise above others.
  14. 6.14Without a glib tongue or good looks, can one escape trouble?This is not advice to cultivate eloquence and looks; it is a lament for the times: when the world values only a sweet tongue and a pretty face, honest people can hardly escape harm.
  15. 6.15Everyone goes out by the door: why not by the right way?Everyone knows to leave by the door rather than climb the wall; the right road in life lies right in front of us, yet people will not take it. It is not that the Way is far from them; they have wandered away themselves.
  16. 6.16Too rough or too polished: which would Confucius prefer?Too much plainness is crude, too much ornament is hollow; only the two in balance make a gentleman. If one must lean, better lean plain: with the base intact, refinement can be added; with the base gone, refinement has nowhere to go.
  17. 6.17Don't crooked people live perfectly well too?People are meant to live upright; the crooked are alive too, not because they deserve it but because they have got away with it by luck.
  18. 6.18Knowing, liking, delighting: what's the difference?Knowing there is this bowl of rice, loving to eat it, and having eaten your fill and being at ease: three levels. The difference is not how much you know, but whether you have actually gained it.
  19. 6.19Was Confucius looking down on people?This is not ranking people, but fitting words to the listener: speak too high and the listener cannot catch it, and harm is done. Teach according to capacity, and climb step by step.
  20. 6.20Did Confucius believe in spirits?The question is not belief or disbelief, but revering and keeping at a distance: first do fully what human affairs require, revere the spirits without petitioning them; in action, the hard part first and the gain after, without reckoning the benefit in advance.
  21. 6.21知者乐水，仁者乐山聪明人通达事理，像水流来流去不卡住
  22. 6.22齐一变至于鲁强弱看的是眼前，离道远近看的是风俗底子
  23. 6.23觚不觚名还在、样子没了，就不算那个东西
  24. 6.24井有仁焉仁者急着救人，但站在井上才救得了人
  25. 6.25博学于文，约之以礼学要广，守要简
  26. 6.26子见南子按礼该见就见，对方不好与己无干
  27. 6.27中庸之为德不过头也不够不着，又天天不变，所以难
  28. 6.28博施济众（能近取譬）博施济众连尧舜都觉得做不全，拿它求仁越求越远

述而第七

  1. 7.1述而不作孔子删诗书、定礼乐，自认只是传旧
  2. 7.2默而识之默记在心、学不嫌够、教不嫌累，本不算圣人的顶点，孔子还说自己没做到，是谦上加谦。今例：夜里还在批作业的老师，被夸时说自己还差得远。
  3. 7.3德之不修四件都是天天要做的功夫，做不到就忧，圣人尚且如此，何况一般人。今例：说好不熬夜又拿起手机，真把手机放远了才算改。
  4. 7.4子之燕居不是孔子的话，是弟子记的样子：闲在家里身子舒展、脸色和悦，不端着也不瘫着。今例：周末在家笑着给阳台的花浇水。
  5. 7.5甚矣吾衰也梦不见周公，不是心不向道了，是身子做不动、行道之心也随之歇了
  6. 7.6志于道，据于德，依于仁，游于艺四步有先后轻重：先立志，再守得住，再不离仁，余力在六艺里游，游也不是闲玩，是让心有处安放。今例：白天上班，晚上练一个钟头字，字不当饭吃，人不会散。
  7. 7.7自行束脩以上束脩是最薄的见面礼，重点不在学费，在“来学”：肯带着礼主动来，孔子没有不教的
  8. 7.8不愤不启，不悱不发上一集谁来都教，这一章讲怎么教：等学的人急到想通想说的时候再点
  9. 7.9子食于有丧者之侧（是日哭则不歌）不是守规矩，是心里真哀伤：挨着办丧事的人吃不出味道，吊丧哭过的那天唱不出来。今例：参加完葬礼开车回家，音乐一响伸手就关了。
  10. 7.10用之则行，舍之则藏出来做不为贪位，收起来不为只顾自己，心里没有非用不可的念头才做得到

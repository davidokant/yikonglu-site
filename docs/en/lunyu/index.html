Xue er (Book 1)

  1. 1.1Learning and practising it in seasonWhat you learned, keep taking out to practise and to do
  2. 1.2Filial and fraternal duty as the root of renFilial and fraternal duty is where practising ren begins
  3. 1.3Clever words and a pleasing faceWatch whether he does it to get the thing done, or only to be liked.
  4. 1.4Three things I examine myself on dailyWhen you act for someone, did you hold anything back? Today: a colleague asks you to look over a plan; you can't be bothered to point out the flaw and say “looks good”.
  5. 1.5Governing a state of a thousand chariotsCheng Yi: “此言至浅……若推其极，尧舜之治亦不过此” — the words are plain, but pushed to the limit, even Yao and Shun governed no differently
  6. 1.6A young man at home is filialThis is about order, not about choosing one and dropping the other
  7. 1.7Honouring the worthy over good looksSomeone who never studied but does all this — Zixia says he has studied
  8. 1.8A gentleman without weight has no dignityZhu Xi takes the words as they stand and does not talk his way around them
  9. 1.9Care at the end, remembrance of the distantPut your heart into the places that are easy to neglect and easy to forget
  10. 1.10Whenever the Master arrives in a stateComing to ask is not the same as handing you the job.
  11. 1.11While the father lives, observe the son's intentWhat is wrong, change now; what can wait, let wait — that is time given to what one cannot bear to let go
  12. 1.12In the use of ritual, harmony is prizedHarmony alone, with nothing to regulate it, does not work either
  13. 1.13Trust that comes close to what is rightThe work happens before you say yes
  14. 1.14A gentleman does not seek to eat his fillNot going hungry on purpose; simply too occupied to mind.
  15. 1.15Poor without flatteryAcknowledge what has been done, then push one step further
  16. 1.16Not troubled that others do not know meThe book opens with “unrecognised and yet not resentful” and closes here; the two ends meet.

Wei zheng (Book 2)

  1. 2.1Governing by virtueNot that decrees are unnecessary, but that the one who governs must first have gained something himself
  2. 2.2Thoughts without crookednessThe “without crookedness” lies in the reader, not entirely in the poems
  3. 2.3Guiding them by decreesPunishment and decree keep people from crime; virtue and ritual move them toward good
  4. 2.4At fifteen I set my heart on learningNot a certificate issued automatically at a certain age, but a road of learning.
  5. 2.5Meng Yizi asks about filial pietyToday: borrowing money to throw a lavish birthday banquet is “doing what one may not”.
  6. 2.6Meng Wubo asks about filial pietyThree readings of one line, side by side
  7. 2.7Ziyou asks about filial pietyBoth readings land on reverence
  8. 2.8Zixia asks about filial piety (the hard part is the face)All the work was done; the face never arrived.
  9. 2.9Hui is not slowWhether someone has learned shows outside the classroom.
  10. 2.10Look at what he doesWhat he does, why he does it, whether he enjoys it
  11. 2.11Reviewing the old to know the newIf you can only recite it, reheating it a hundred times still gives the same bowl.
  12. 2.12The gentleman is not a vesselVirtue made whole first, then it can be used anywhere — not learning a hundred trades
  13. 2.13Act first, then speakYou still speak — only afterwards.
  14. 2.14Inclusive, not partisanSame dishes on the table, different reasons for the dinner.
  15. 2.15Learning without thinkingOne is empty, the other hangs in the air
  16. 2.16Working at strange doctrinesFollowing Zhu Xi: burrowing into it is what does harm.
  17. 2.17To know what you knowAdmitting you do not know is where it starts.
  18. 2.18Zizhang studies to gain officeConfucius promises nothing; he turns seeking office into a lesson in being a person.
  19. 2.19Raise up the straightThe hard part is seeing who is straight
  20. 2.20Approach them with dignityJi Kangzi asks for a method; Confucius answers with duty. Treating duty as a method goes wrong again.
  21. 2.21Why do you not take part in government?The words are a pretext, and the point is also true.
  22. 2.22A person without trustworthinessWithout it, the ox is an ox and the cart is a cart; they never connect.
  23. 2.23Can ten generations be known?He watched two handovers and saw what stayed and what changed.
  24. 2.24Sacrificing to spirits not one's ownTaking gifts to the home of a boss you have nothing to do with; saying nothing in the meeting.

Ba yi (Book 3)

  1. 3.1Eight rows of dancers in his courtyardFour extra rows of dancers measure how hard a heart has become
  2. 3.2The three families clear away to the YongThey didn't even understand the song they sang
  3. 3.3A person without humaneness — what of ritual?Fill the eight rows, sing the emperor's ode — if the person is wrong, ritual and music can do nothing with him.
  4. 3.4Lin Fang asks about the root of ritualWhen both sides are off, lean towards the root
  5. 3.5The Yi and Di have rulersThe rebuke is aimed not at the Yi and Di but at his own people
  6. 3.6The Ji sacrifice to Mount TaiIt was the Ji who needed saving, not Mount Tai
  7. 3.7The gentleman does not competeThe target does not lie; what is contested is whether your own hand is steady.
  8. 3.8The painting comes after the white groundIf the ground is not smooth, every colour you put on comes out blotchy.
  9. 3.9Not enough recordsConfucius could tell it, but would not rest on his word alone
  10. 3.10The di sacrifice after the libationLooking back, the records and the people are gone; looking around, the rite is there but the heart is not.
  11. 3.11Someone asks about the di sacrificeSaying “I don't know” does not mean he didn't know.
  12. 3.12Sacrifice as if presentThe form of the rite can be laid out by the book; the heart in it has to be brought by the one who sacrifices.
  13. 3.13Better to court the stoveDo the thing right and everyone is easy to deal with; do it wrong and being on good terms with anyone won't help.
  14. 3.14Zhou looked to the two dynastiesFollowing Zhou means following the work of improving on predecessors bit by bit
  15. 3.15In the Grand Temple he asked about everythingThirty years in, you still double-check.
  16. 3.16In archery, piercing the hide is not the pointThe contest is in what can be trained, not in what you were born with.
  17. 3.17The sheep for announcing the new moonThe sheep marks the rite; keep the marker and the rite can still be recognised and restored
  18. 3.18Serving one's lord with full ritualNot a bit extra — ritual simply requires this; Confucius says only “people take it for flattery”, with no insult back
  19. 3.19The lord employs his ministers with ritualEach side looks only at its own end; Duke Ding was the ruler, and his question was answered with his own half
  20. 3.20The Guanju: joy without excessNot too little feeling, but feeling full without going over
  21. 3.21Duke Ai asks about the altar of the soilThe three “nots” are not “never mind” but a heavy rebuke, to make him careful in future
  22. 3.22Guan Zhong's capacity was smallConfucius does not say outright where the smallness lies; it shows in the luxury and usurpation
  23. 3.23The Master speaks of music to the Grand Music Master of LuFirst together, then harmonious, each part clear within the harmony, linked to the end
  24. 3.24The border warden of Yi asks to see himA minor official saw Confucius's weight at first sight
  25. 3.25The Shao is perfectly beautiful and perfectly goodThe difference is in how it came: one was yielded, one was taken by force
  26. 3.26High position without generosityIt is not that it looks bad; there is nothing left to look at

Li ren (Book 4)

  1. 4.1Living among the humane is beautifulChoose the wrong place to live and what you lose is not the house but the heart that tells right from wrong
  2. 4.2The inhumane cannot long endure hardshipLong hardship makes people overstep, long pleasure makes them indulge; whether outside things can take a person away depends on whether the original heart is there
  3. 4.3Only the humane can love and hate othersEveryone can like and dislike; the “can” lies in doing it rightly, which requires a heart that is not tied to anything
  4. 4.4Set your heart on humaneness and there is no evilResolve does not guarantee no mistakes; it guarantees no intention to do wrong
  5. 4.5Wealth and rank are what people wantDo not take wealth that should not come, and do not dodge an undeserved poverty by crooked means; the big choices and the small daily work make each other
  6. 4.6I have not seen one who loves humanenessWhere the heart goes, the strength goes; the hard part is not strength but willingness
  7. 4.7People's faults each follow their kindWhether a fault leans too generous or too mean shows whether one is humane

雍也第六 · chapter 20Did Confucius believe in spirits?

What this chapter says

The question is not belief or disbelief, but revering and keeping at a distance: first do fully what human affairs require, revere the spirits without petitioning them; in action, the hard part first and the gain after, without reckoning the benefit in advance.

白Plain Chinese

樊迟问怎样算明智。孔子说：专心做人该做的正事，对鬼神敬重而保持距离，就可以叫明智了。又问怎样算仁。孔子说：仁者先下难做的功夫，后想得到的收获，就可以叫仁了。

常The common reading

Did Confucius believe in ghosts and spirits?

注Key words in the commentary

“民，亦人也。获，谓得也” — “min” also means people; “huo” means gain; “专用力于人道之所宜，而不惑于鬼神之不可知，知者之事也。先其事之所难，而后其效之所得，仁者之心也” — to put one's effort wholly into what is fitting in the human way and not be confused by the unknowable spirits is the business of the wise; to put first what is hard in a task and only after think of what it yields is the heart of the humane; “此必因樊迟之失而告之” — this must have been said in view of Fan Chi's failings.

说What other commentators say

Master Cheng: “人多信鬼神，惑也。而不信者又不能敬，能敬能远，可谓知矣” — many people believe in spirits, which is confusion; those who do not believe cannot revere them. To be able both to revere and to keep at a distance may be called wisdom; “先难，克己也。以所难为先，而不计所获，仁也” — putting the difficult first is mastering oneself; to take the hard as first without counting the gain is humaneness. Lü: “当务为急，不求所难知；力行所知，不惮所难为” — put urgency on present duties and do not seek what is hard to know; practise vigorously what you know and do not shrink from what is hard to do.

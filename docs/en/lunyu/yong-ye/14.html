雍也第六 · chapter 14Without a glib tongue or good looks, can one escape trouble?

What this chapter says

This is not advice to cultivate eloquence and looks; it is a lament for the times: when the world values only a sweet tongue and a pretty face, honest people can hardly escape harm.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：没有祝鮀那样能说会道，只有宋朝那样一副好相貌，在如今这世上怕是难保平安了！

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

常The common reading

Without eloquence or good looks, is trouble unavoidable?

注Key words in the commentary

“鮀，徒何反” — “鮀” is read tuó; “祝，宗庙之官。鮀，卫大夫，字子鱼，有口才。朝，宋公子，有美色” — “zhu” is an officer of the ancestral temple; Tuo was a grandee of Wey, courtesy name Ziyu, a skilled talker; Zhao was a prince of Song, handsome; “言衰世好谀悦色，非此难免，盖伤之也” — it says a declining age loves flattery and good looks, and without these one can hardly escape: he was lamenting it.

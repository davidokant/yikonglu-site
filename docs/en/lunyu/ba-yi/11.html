Ba yi (Book 3) · chapter 11Someone asks about the di sacrifice

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: Saying “I don't know” does not mean he didn't know. Zhu Xi's commentary: “示，与视同” — shi is the same as “see”; “指其掌，弟子记夫子言此而自指其掌” — the disciples record that he pointed to his own palm as he said this; “非或人之所及也” — beyond what the questioner could reach.

白Plain Chinese

有人问禘祭的道理。孔子说：我不知道。懂得这道理的人，治理天下，就像把东西摆在这里看一样吧！一边说，一边指着自己的手掌。

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

注Key words in the commentary

“示，与视同” — shi is the same as “see”; “指其掌，弟子记夫子言此而自指其掌” — the disciples record that he pointed to his own palm as he said this; “非或人之所及也” — beyond what the questioner could reach; “不王不禘之法，又鲁之所当讳者，故以不知答之” — the rule that only kings perform di was something Lu had to avoid mentioning, so he answered “I do not know”; “岂真有所不知也哉” — did he really not know?

Compared with Zhu Xi

CommentaryPointRelationConfidenceNote
钱穆《论语新解》示differsfairly sureQian follows 寘, placing, as if set in one's palm; Zhu follows 视, as plain as looking at one's palm. Neither thinks Confucius truly did not know the meaning of the di.
杨伯峻《论语译注》示word sensesettledYang mainly takes 示 as placing, laying out, also noting “seeing”; Zhu takes 示 as 视, clear and easy. Both hold “I do not know” avoids speaking of Lu's usurpation, not real ignorance.

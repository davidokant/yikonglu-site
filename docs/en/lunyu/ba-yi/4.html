Ba yi (Book 3) · chapter 4Lin Fang asks about the root of ritual

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: Lavishness and frugality both miss ritual; when both sides are off, lean toward the root. Today: at an elder's birthday, one table is crammed with dishes and everyone stares at their phones; another family has made just a bowl of noodles and sits with the old man until he has finished. The common view is “The simpler the better”; Yikonglu's reading puts the weight elsewhere.

白Plain Chinese

林放问礼的根本。孔子说：你这个问题意义重大啊！礼，与其奢侈，宁可俭朴；丧事，与其仪节周全，宁可内心悲痛。

常The common reading

The simpler the better.

注Key words in the commentary

“易，治也” — yi means arranged, as in tending fields; “节文习熟，而无哀痛惨怛之实” — the forms practised smoothly with no real grief; “礼贵得中……二者皆未合礼” — ritual values the mean, and both extremes fall short; “必先有质而后有文，则质乃礼之本也” — substance must come before form, so substance is the root of ritual.

说What other commentators say

Fan Zuyu: “祭与其敬不足而礼有余也，不若礼不足而敬有余也” — in sacrifice, better too little ceremony and enough reverence than the reverse; Yang Shi: “礼始诸饮食，故汙尊而抔饮” — ritual began with eating and drinking, with hollows for cups and hands for drinking; “周衰，世方以文灭质” — as Zhou declined, form was smothering substance.

钱穆《论语新解》samefairly sureBoth: substance is ritual's root; frugality and grief are better than lavishness and smoothness, yet form and substance must still balance. Qian lists two glosses of 易, slight and well managed, choosing the latter in translation; Zhu glosses 易 as managing.

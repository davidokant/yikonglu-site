Ba yi (Book 3) · chapter 5The Yi and Di have rulers

What this chapter says

The rebuke is aimed not at the Yi and Di but at his own people. Today: an old family firm where each branch keeps its own books.

白Plain Chinese

孔子说：夷狄尚且有君长，不像中原各国，有君却名存实亡。

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

常The common reading

Contempt for the barbarians.

注Key words in the commentary

“亡，古无字，通用” — wang is the old form of wu, “without” (Wu Yu).

说What other commentators say

Cheng Yi: “夷狄且有君长，不如诸夏之僭乱，反无上下之分也” — even the Yi and Di have rulers and chiefs, unlike the Xia states in their usurpation and disorder, with no distinction of high and low; Yin Tun: “亡，非实亡也，虽有之，不能尽其道尔” — “without” does not mean they truly lack rulers, but that they have them and do not fulfil the role.

Compared with Zhu Xi

CommentaryPointRelationConfidenceNote
杨伯峻《论语译注》differssettledYang's main text: barbarians with rulers are still not as good as the Xia states without them; Zhu cites Cheng and Yin: barbarians still have rulers while the Xia states usurp and are in disorder, lacking even the distinction of ranks. Yang notes the latter is possible.
钱穆《论语新解》differsfairly sureOpposite readings: Qian takes it that barbarians with rulers are still not equal to the Xia states without them, stressing ritual; Zhu cites Cheng and Yin, using barbarians who still have rulers to set off the Xia states, so usurped they seem to have none.

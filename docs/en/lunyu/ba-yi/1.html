Ba yi (Book 3) · chapter 1Eight rows of dancers in his courtyard

白Plain Chinese

孔子谈到季氏时说：他在自家庭院里用天子规格的八行舞，这样的事他都狠得下心做，还有什么事他做不出来！

A plain modern-Chinese rendering following Yikonglu's reading.

常The common reading

“If this can be borne, what cannot?” = I can't stand it any more.

注Key words in the commentary

“佾，舞列也” — yi is a row of dancers; “天子八，诸侯六，大夫四，士二” — the Son of Heaven eight, lords six, ministers four, officers two (two accounts of dancers per row, “未详孰是” — unclear which is right); “孔子言其此事尚忍为之，则何事不可忍为” — if he could bring himself to do this, what could he not bring himself to do (ren = the Ji's hardness of heart); “或曰：忍，容忍也” — another reading: ren means to tolerate, given alongside.

家What other commentators say

Fan Zuyu: “降杀以两……不可以毫发僭差” — the ranks step down by two and must not be exceeded by a hair; Xie Liangzuo: “季氏忍此矣，则虽弑父与君，亦何所惮而不为” — if the Ji could do this, what would stop them even from killing father or lord?

读How Yikonglu reads it

Four extra rows of dancers measure how hard a heart has become. Today: sitting in the boss's chair at the meeting.

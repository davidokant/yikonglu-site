Ba yi (Book 3) · chapter 12Sacrifice as if present

What this chapter says

Yikonglu's reading: The form of the rite can be laid out by the book; the heart in it has to be brought by the one who sacrifices. A common question is “Did Confucius believe in spirits” — the line above is Yikonglu's answer.

白Plain Chinese

祭祀祖先，就像祖先在眼前；祭祀神，就像神在眼前。孔子说：我不亲自参加的祭祀，就跟没有祭一样。

常The common reading

Did Confucius believe in spirits?

注Key words in the commentary

Cheng Yi: “祭，祭先祖也；祭神，祭外神也；祭先主于孝，祭神主于敬” — ji is sacrifice to ancestors, jishen to other spirits; ancestors call for filial piety, spirits for reverence; “此门人记孔子祭祀之诚意” — the disciples record Confucius's sincerity in sacrifice; yu (take part) in the departing tone; “此心缺然，如未尝祭也” — the heart feels the gap, as if one had never sacrificed.

说What other commentators say

Fan Zuyu: “有其诚则有其神，无其诚则无其神” — with sincerity there are spirits, without it there are none; “诚为实，礼为虚也” — sincerity is the substance, ritual the empty form.

Compared with Zhu Xi

CommentaryPointRelationConfidenceNote
杨伯峻《论语译注》differssettledYang renders 如不祭 freely as “I do not ask another to sacrifice for me”; Zhu: if another performs it, it is done, but one's own sincerity is missing, as if not done. Both take 与 as taking part.
钱穆《论语新解》samefairly sureBoth: the first two lines are disciples recording Confucius's sincerity in sacrifice; “if I do not take part” means unable to attend and having another stand in. Both stress personal sincerity, not objects and forms alone.
